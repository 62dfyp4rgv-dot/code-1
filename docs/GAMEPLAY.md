# Gameplay guide

## Loop
Pick a country → read the **Objectives** tab (6 victory paths) → run the state by spending **political capital (PC)**
on policy changes, setting budget priorities, directing research, trading, signing treaties, and responding to events and
crises. Speed buttons run 1/4/12/40 days per second; the game pauses on decisions and on war declarations involving you, and pauses + autosaves when the app is backgrounded.
There is no single "win" button: the scenario ends after 8–10 years (or on collapse/capitulation) and grades your
performance across six paths.

## HUD and map
- Top: country chip, date, speed/play, inbox. Pills: **PC**, **Influence (Inf)**, **Approval**, GDP and more (scroll sideways).
- Map: drag to pan, pinch or double-tap to zoom, tap a country for its card, tap the target button to centre on yourself.
  Modes: Political, Relations, Economy, Stability, Military, Resources, Trade flows (per commodity).
- Bottom tabs: **Map · Nation · Economy · Diplomacy · Military · More** (research, intelligence, inbox, statistics, objectives, saves, settings, help).

## Nation
Eleven policy levers: tax rate, spending rate, tariff, policy interest rate, regulation, environmental regulation,
clean-energy priority, immigration, press freedom, surveillance, conscription. Each change costs PC, shows a *preview* of
expected effects first, and takes months to act (lags). Budget screen splits spending across categories; industrial focus biases sectors.
Larger moves cost more PC, so plan which levers matter.

## Advisor
The ★ pill at the top (or More → Advisor) turns your numbers into a short list: urgent decisions, peace offers, war exhaustion, inflation, reserves, stability, elections, civil conflict, ideas for growth. Items are sorted by urgency and open the right screen when tapped. It only reads the game; it never acts for you.

## World variation
New game → *World variation*: **Real world** (default) starts from the 2025 snapshot; **Some** and **Wild** shuffle starting strengths, add booms, long slumps and new rivalries between neighbours. The same seed and setting always give the same world.

## Civil conflict and shifting alliances
A state that is unstable (< 30 %), restless (> 50 %) and strained can slide into a civil conflict: it drains output, devastates regions and pushes refugees into neighbouring countries until a settlement, exhaustion, or regime change ends it. Alliances and defence pacts lapse after a year in which the partners' opinions of each other stay negative.

## National projects
Nation → Projects: 16 multi-year investments (high-speed rail, universal healthcare, grid modernisation, civil nuclear, space programme, cyber command, semiconductor fabs, irrigation, education reform, anti-corruption agency, border defence, blue-water navy, industrial zones, pandemic preparedness, carbon capture, digital government). Each needs conditions (development, research, corruption, water stress…), costs political capital to start and a share of GDP per year (borrowed), and grants permanent modifiers when finished. Two at a time; cancelling forfeits what was spent.

## Economy
Watch **GDP growth, inflation vs target, unemployment vs natural rate, deficit/debt, reserves**. Energy and food
imports expose you to price shocks and chokepoints; sanctions and embargoes cut trade both ways.
High debt + low reserves + weak confidence ⇒ crisis. The central bank follows a rule you can override at a political cost.

## Research
Queue up to several technologies; points come from R&D funding and human capital; followers get cheaper catch-up. Techs give
modifiers (productivity, energy, military quality, health, climate adaptation, state capacity).

## Diplomacy
Select a country → propose (trade deal, defence pact, alliance, non-aggression, join organisation, sanctions request…). The
panel lists *why* a partner would accept or refuse (opinion, shared rivals, power balance, trust) and what it costs you. Organisations
have membership criteria and need member consent. The **Global Assembly** votes on resolutions (forecast shown; veto powers block).

## Military & war
Allocate budget to land/air/naval and readiness; formations appear on the map for you and (through fog of war) as estimates for rivals.
War: declare (casus belli matters; allies cannot be attacked), set stances per enemy (attack/defend), direct formations, watch **supply**
— unsupplied armies stall. Straits require sea power; landings are costly. Occupation, devastation and exhaustion drive the war score;
propose peace with terms and see acceptance factors. Nuclear powers are deterred from being attacked but escalation has costs.

## Intelligence
Build networks first (more networks ⇒ sharper numbers about others), then run operations: influence, disinformation, sabotage,
cyber, technology acquisition, support for opposition, counter-intelligence. Failure can expose you and damage relations.

## Events
~80 authored crises and opportunities fire from the *state of the world*, not a script. Many have 2–3 options with trade-offs. An unanswered decision expires after its deadline (default 30 days) and the default option is applied.

## Winning
Six paths: **Prosperity** (+35 % GDP per head, poverty −20 %, development up), **Economic leadership**, **Diplomatic influence**,
**Scientific advancement**, **Stability & welfare**, **Security & strength**. Grade: Triumph (≥4 paths), Success (≥2), Qualified success (1), Muddled through (0).
Sandbox continuation is available after the end screen. Fifteen achievements track optional challenges.

## Tips
Start in the Objectives tab; fix inflation before elections; diversify energy imports; don't fight on two fronts; spend PC on
long-lag, high-value levers early; keep reserves above two months of imports.
