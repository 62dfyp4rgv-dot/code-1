# Scenario prose samples

A representative sample of the 85 situations shown to a simulated player during a played career (day 560, 96 matches): up to 8 that cited remembered history, then others in order. Choices were picked at random. `Memory` lines are generated from ledger events the character can actually cite.

## Stuck Between Floors with Ilja Dragunov  _(day 135, rivalry)_

> The hotel elevator shudders to a stop one floor short of the lobby, and you are alone with Ilja Dragunov for eleven minutes. No crowd, no cameras, and no script for what you are supposed to do with your hands. "Do not say anything," Ilja says, eyes on the floor numbers.
> The feud has always had an audience to perform for. Take the audience away and you find out whether anything is left underneath.

Memory callbacks shown: "You betrayed me. I haven't forgotten, and I never will."

Choices offered: [Say what the feud has actually cost you] [Keep the act going though nobody is watching] [Needle Ilja about something that actually stings] [Offer your water bottle and stay quiet]

Chosen: **Say what the feud has actually cost you** → You say it plainly: the sleep, the bruises, the way the road eats everything. Ilja listens with arms folded, then admits to three of the four, and the elevator suddenly feels bigger.

## Bryce Black Wants the Belt  _(day 153, championship)_

> Bryce Black has put your title in his sights with a signed petition from the locker room. He calls himself the next champion and demands a match.
> You could ignore it, but the audience is already picking sides. Whatever you decide, the belt on your shoulder will take the blame or the credit.

Memory callbacks shown: "You beat me clean twice. No excuses."

Choices offered: [Accept the challenge and defend your title] [Stall: make Bryce earn it in other matches first] [Accept, but inside a steel cage] [Promise to retain your title against everyone]

Chosen: **Promise to retain your title against everyone** → You tell the world nobody is taking your belt. It is a gutsy claim, and the cameras love it.

## Two People, One Hotel Fitness Room with One Treadmill  _(day 154, training)_

> You arrive early to work on a conditioning circuit, and Bryce Black is already there. Neither of you says anything, and neither of you leaves, which leaves the hotel fitness room with one treadmill feeling half the size it was a minute ago.
> It would be easy to turn this into a cold war or into a fight. It could also turn into the most honest hour either of you has had all month.

Memory callbacks shown: "The record says I own you. Check it."

Choices offered: [Suggest working the drill together] [Quietly try to outwork Bryce] [Pick up your bag and train somewhere else] [Ask Bryce for notes on one specific move]

Chosen: **Suggest working the drill together** → Bryce considers it for ten seconds, then drops his bag and comes over. The hour is silent, hard and strangely good, and you both learn things you would not have learned alone.

## Bryce Black Asks for Notes  _(day 187, rivalry)_

> Bryce Black finds you in the production office after hours and asks the one question nobody in this business asks an enemy. "I have a dive that ends in a bad landing," Bryce says. "Tell me the truth."
> The respect between you has grown a lot since the feud began, and it makes the request feel harder to refuse and harder to trust. Teaching a rival is a strange kind of gift.

Memory callbacks shown: "You beat me clean 3 times. No excuses."

Choices offered: [Give your honest notes, all of them] [Give notes that quietly make it worse] [Trade notes: you want something in return] [Say no: the feud is not a classroom]

Chosen: **Give notes that quietly make it worse** → Bryce tests the fix in the empty ring first and sees through it in a minute. The silence afterwards is long, and the trust you had is gone.

## The Cold Shoulder from Ilja Dragunov  _(day 209, betrayal)_

> Right after the card, Ilja Dragunov leaves a room the moment you walk in, and nobody comments on it. The silence is perfectly polite, and that is what makes it unbearable.
> Whoever struck first, the break is still the loudest thing in every room you share. What nobody tells you is how long a person can stay careful around the one who broke something.

Memory callbacks shown: "You betrayed me. I haven't forgotten, and I never will."

Choices offered: [Take it quietly and give him the room] [Corner Ilja and demand to talk] [Leave a handwritten note and then leave it alone] [Tell the room Ilja is being petty]

Chosen: **Take it quietly and give him the room** → You eat standing up and keep out of the way. Ilja registers the effort and gives you nothing for it, which is a kind of fairness.

## Someone Talked to the Press  _(day 210, media)_

> Casey Lindqvist (Backstage Wire) calls before the piece runs, which is more courtesy than most give. It is a two-part radio special titled "A Locker Room Divided", and Ilja Dragunov is quoted all the way through.
> "You get a reply, of course," Casey says, like someone who has done this many times and enjoys it less each year.

Memory callbacks shown: "One loss to you. Tonight I fix that."

Choices offered: [Give a short, calm reply and no adjectives] [Give Casey your whole side, on the record] [Call Ilja before saying anything to the press] [Decline to comment and let the weekend pass]

Chosen: **Decline to comment and let the weekend pass** → The piece prints with "declined to comment" in grey type and gets the usual few days of attention. The fans who know you take your silence as dignity; the ones who do not take it as an admission.

## Bryce Black Posts a Not-So-Subtle Jab  _(day 238, social)_

> Bryce Black has posted a sarcastic "thoughts and prayers" for your last match with no names attached, but everybody knows who it is for. It has already collected a trending tag, and your own mentions are demanding a reply.
> Bryce wants a response. The only choice is whether you give him one, and in what form.

Memory callbacks shown: "3 clean victories over you. Not one fluke among them."

Choices offered: [Reply with a razor-sharp clapback] [Respond with a gracious, kind message] [Answer in the ring, not online] [Mute him and say nothing]

Chosen: **Respond with a gracious, kind message** → A warm reply that makes Bryce look petty. The crowd sees a mature competitor, and Bryce sees an opponent who will not be baited.

## Pierce Wolfe Says You Are Predictable  _(day 253, press)_

> Pierce Wolfe told Casey Lindqvist (Backstage Wire) on a radio call-in that you telegraph your best move in the second minute. Casey reads it back to you with the glee of a person delivering a sealed letter.
> It stings because it is specific, and it is specific because Pierce has spent real time in your corner of the film room. The question is whether to deny it, adjust to it or sell it.

Memory callbacks shown: "You beat me clean 3 times. No excuses."

Choices offered: [Say nothing and quietly change the pattern] [Agree publicly and dare Pierce to stop it] [Deny it flatly and call it the words of a jealous person] [Thank Pierce for the free scouting report]

Chosen: **Agree publicly and dare Pierce to stop it** → Admitting the weakness takes the weapon out of Pierce's hand, and the crowd goes wild at the confidence. In the ring you hit the pattern and it works anyway.

## A Story from the Old Days  _(day 3, mentor)_

> During a rain delay at the airport, Randy Orton starts telling a story about the time a title run ended before it began. It does not sound like a lesson at first, just a story, but by the end you realize it was meant for you.
> The veteran watches you the whole time, curious to see what you take from it.

Choices offered: [Listen closely and take it in] [Ask the hard question underneath it] [Offer a story of your own in return] [Make an excuse and slip away]

Chosen: **Ask the hard question underneath it** → Randy pauses, then answers honestly. It is the truest thing you have heard all year.

## Tryout at a Former Bowling Alley  _(day 15, debut)_

> The tryout is held in a former bowling alley, and the ring has clearly been assembled that morning. Samira Okoye sits at the edge of the apron. Also in the room: forty other hopefuls.
> You get ninety seconds of ring time and a promise that someone will call. Most people in the room will never hear from anyone.

Choices offered: [Run the basics with perfect execution] [Go for the highlight move] [Ask for a few seconds on the mic as well] [Spend your savings on a hotel night afterwards]

Chosen: **Run the basics with perfect execution** → Every bump, every lock-up, every roll-through is textbook. Nobody gasps, but nobody frowns either, and your name ends up on the callback list.

## The Agent Wants a Name  _(day 32, betrayal)_

> Nadia Petrova lines everyone up after a prank that went bad and ruined a costume. "Somebody is going to tell me who did this, or the entire roster takes a fine."
> You saw it, and the culprit is Elias Lindgren. Nobody else knows you saw.

Choices offered: [Give the agent the name] [Cover for him and say you saw nothing] [Take the blame yourself] [Make the culprit confess, quietly]

Chosen: **Cover for him and say you saw nothing** → Nobody talks and everyone takes the hit. Elias owes you one.

## The Conditioning Test  _(day 36, training)_

> Walter Grimes sets up a shuttle run timed to the bell with a stopwatch and a face that gives nothing away. By the end, the numbers on the clipboard confirm something you already suspected: your legs fail long before your lungs do.
> A wrestler can hide a weakness for a few minutes, but never for a full television hour. The coach slides the sheet across the table. "Read it," Walter says. "Then tell me it is wrong."

Choices offered: [Double your conditioning work for the next month] [Change your style to rest in the match] [Hire the coach to build a proper plan] [Shrug it off: the test is not the match]

Chosen: **Hire the coach to build a proper plan** → Walter drafts a plan that includes sleep, food and a schedule that actually accounts for travel. The price is steep and the plan is realiztic, and the first week is a revelation.

## A Training Partner Worth Keeping  _(day 42, alliance)_

> Ilja Dragunov suggests training together, with a focus on heavy conditioning blocks. The deal comes with two hours after every show.
> Every trainer on the card says the same thing: you improve fastest next to someone better than you. You are not sure which of you that is.

Choices offered: [Commit to a full training block] [Agree to one session a week] [Swap training secrets instead] [Pass: you work better alone]

Chosen: **Commit to a full training block** → Three weeks in, you notice the change. You are stronger, sharper and a little more tired than you have ever been.

## Ilja Dragunov Cleans Out a Locker  _(day 43, contract)_

> The release was processed that morning, and Ilja Dragunov is packing on the loading dock with a bag of boots. Everyone else in the building seems to have discovered urgent business elsewhere.
> The goodbye arrives with a company envelope. You still have a contract, and the luck feels like a stone in your pocket.

Choices offered: [Help carry the bag to the car] [Take Ilja out for a proper dinner] [Go to the office and argue for a second look] [Say a quick goodbye and keep your distance]

Chosen: **Help carry the bag to the car** → You say little, because there is little to say, and you carry the bag as though it weighed more. Ilja grips your arm once at the car door. "Don't let them change you," Ilja says, and drives away without looking back.

## The Referee Who Covered for You  _(day 51, backstage)_

> Halfway through the match you blundered and lost your place with a hold half applied. The referee, a veteran in a worn shirt, guided you out of the dead air through the earpiece, and the crowd never smelled a thing.
> You find the referee afterward in the corridor, retying a shoe and humming. "Happens to everybody," he says before you can speak. He has done this for thirty years and has never once been thanked for the saves nobody noticed.

Choices offered: [Buy the referee a proper dinner] [Ask him to watch the tape with you] [Apologize at length and swear it will not happen again] [Shrug it off as part of the night]

Chosen: **Ask him to watch the tape with you** → The two of you sit in a dark production room and pick the match apart, from the first collar-and-elbow to the last handshake. He is blunt, patient and right about most things.

## The Prank in Catering  _(day 55, debut)_

> Someone has done it to you in catering: a note on your gear that says "ask me about my finisher". Knox Calloway is leaning on the wall with the face of a person who knows nothing and invented all of it.
> "Welcome to the business," he says to no one in particular. Half the room is waiting to see what you do, since this old tradition has a way of revealing character.

Choices offered: [Laugh it off and play along] [Plan a better prank on Knox Calloway] [Ignore it and keep your eyes on your gear] [Complain to a producer about the hazing]

Chosen: **Plan a better prank on Knox Calloway** → Your retaliation is neat and totally harmless. Knox Calloway bows to the crowd and the whole room declares you a keeper.
